# lichess_themes (47 rows)

## Lichess puzzle themes

### Advanced pawn
`theme:advancedPawn` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/advancedPawn)

Advanced pawn: One of your pawns is deep into the opponent position, maybe threatening to promote.

### Anastasia's mate
`theme:anastasiaMate` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/anastasiaMate)

Anastasia's mate: A knight and rook or queen team up to trap the opposing king between the side of the board and a friendly piece.

### Arabian mate
`theme:arabianMate` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/arabianMate)

Arabian mate: A knight and a rook team up to trap the opposing king on a corner of the board.

### Attacking f2 or f7
`theme:attackingF2F7` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/attackingF2F7)

Attacking f2 or f7: An attack focusing on the f2 or f7 pawn, such as in the fried liver opening.

### Attraction
`theme:attraction` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/attraction)

Attraction: An exchange or sacrifice encouraging or forcing an opponent piece to a square that allows a follow-up tactic.

### Back rank mate
`theme:backRankMate` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/backRankMate)

Back rank mate: Checkmate the king on the home rank, when it is trapped there by its own pieces.

### Balestra mate
`theme:balestraMate` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/balestraMate)

Balestra mate: A bishop delivers the checkmate, while a queen blocks the remaining escape squares

### Blind Swine mate
`theme:blindSwineMate` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/blindSwineMate)

Blind Swine mate: Two rooks team up to mate the king in an area of 2 by 2 squares.

### Bishop endgame
`theme:bishopEndgame` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/bishopEndgame)

Bishop endgame: An endgame with only bishops and pawns.

### Boden's mate
`theme:bodenMate` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/bodenMate)

Boden's mate: Two attacking bishops on criss-crossing diagonals deliver mate to a king obstructed by friendly pieces.

### Castling
`theme:castling` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/castling)

Castling: Bring the king to safety, and deploy the rook for attack.

### Capture the defender
`theme:capturingDefender` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/capturingDefender)

Capture the defender: Removing a piece that is critical to defence of another piece, allowing the now undefended piece to be captured on a following move.

### Collinear move
`theme:collinearMove` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/collinearMove)

Collinear move: Two opposing pieces face each other, and one slides along the line of attack without capturing the enemy piece.

### Discovered check
`theme:discoveredCheck` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/discoveredCheck)

Discovered check: Move a piece to reveal a check from a hidden attacking piece, which often leads to a decisive advantage.

### Double bishop mate
`theme:doubleBishopMate` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/doubleBishopMate)

Double bishop mate: Two attacking bishops on adjacent diagonals deliver mate to a king obstructed by friendly pieces.

### Dovetail mate
`theme:dovetailMate` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/dovetailMate)

Dovetail mate: A queen delivers mate to an adjacent king, whose only two escape squares are obstructed by friendly pieces.

### Kingside attack
`theme:kingsideAttack` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/kingsideAttack)

Kingside attack: An attack of the opponent's king, after they castled on the king side.

### Clearance
`theme:clearance` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/clearance)

Clearance: A move, often with tempo, that clears a square, file or diagonal for a follow-up tactical idea.

### Defensive move
`theme:defensiveMove` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/defensiveMove)

Defensive move: A precise move or sequence of moves that is needed to avoid losing material or another advantage.

### Deflection
`theme:deflection` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/deflection)

Deflection: A move that distracts an opposing piece from another duty that it performs, such as guarding a key square. Sometimes also called "overloading".

### Discovered attack
`theme:discoveredAttack` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/discoveredAttack)

Discovered attack: Moving a piece (such as a knight), that previously blocked an attack by a long range piece (such as a rook), out of the way of that piece.

### Double check
`theme:doubleCheck` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/doubleCheck)

Double check: Checking with two pieces at once, as a result of a discovered attack where both the moving piece and the unveiled piece attack the opponent's king.

### Epaulette mate
`theme:epauletteMate` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/epauletteMate)

Epaulette mate: Two adjacent escape squares for a checked king are occupied by other pieces.

### Exposed king
`theme:exposedKing` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/exposedKing)

Exposed king: A tactic involving a king with few defenders around it, often leading to checkmate.

### Hanging piece
`theme:hangingPiece` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/hangingPiece)

Hanging piece: A tactic involving an opponent piece being undefended or insufficiently defended and free to capture.

### Interference
`theme:interference` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/interference)

Interference: Moving a piece between two opponent pieces to leave one or both opponent pieces undefended, such as a knight on a defended square between two rooks.

### Intermezzo
`theme:intermezzo` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/intermezzo)

Intermezzo: Instead of playing the expected move, first interpose another move posing an immediate threat that the opponent must answer. Also known as "Zwischenzug" or "In between".

### Kill box mate
`theme:killBoxMate` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/killBoxMate)

Kill box mate: A rook is next to the enemy king and supported by a queen that also blocks the king's escape squares. The rook and the queen catch the enemy king in a 3 by 3 "kill box".

### Swallow's tail mate
`theme:swallowstailMate` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/swallowstailMate)

Swallow's tail mate: A checkmate pattern that visually resembles the appearance of a swallow’s tail, similar to a V shape.

### Triangle mate
`theme:triangleMate` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/triangleMate)

Triangle mate: The queen and rook, one square away from the enemy king, are on the same rank or file, separated by one square, forming a triangle.

### Vuković mate
`theme:vukovicMate` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/vukovicMate)

Vuković mate: A rook and knight team up to mate the king. The rook delivers mate while supported by a third piece, and the knight is used to block the king's escape squares.

### Knight endgame
`theme:knightEndgame` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/knightEndgame)

Knight endgame: An endgame with only knights and pawns.

### Opera mate
`theme:operaMate` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/operaMate)

Opera mate: Check the king with a rook and use a bishop to defend the rook.

### Pawn endgame
`theme:pawnEndgame` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/pawnEndgame)

Pawn endgame: An endgame with only pawns.

### Pin
`theme:pin` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/pin)

Pin: A tactic involving pins, where a piece is unable to move without revealing an attack on a higher value piece.

### Queen endgame
`theme:queenEndgame` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/queenEndgame)

Queen endgame: An endgame with only queens and pawns.

### Queen and Rook
`theme:queenRookEndgame` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/queenRookEndgame)

Queen and Rook: An endgame with only queens, rooks and pawns.

### Queenside attack
`theme:queensideAttack` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/queensideAttack)

Queenside attack: An attack of the opponent's king, after they castled on the queen side.

### Quiet move
`theme:quietMove` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/quietMove)

Quiet move: A move that does not check, capture, or create an immediate threat to capture. Instead, it prepares a hidden and unavoidable threat for a later move.

### Rook endgame
`theme:rookEndgame` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/rookEndgame)

Rook endgame: An endgame with only rooks and pawns.

### Sacrifice
`theme:sacrifice` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/sacrifice)

Sacrifice: A tactic involving giving up material in the short-term, to gain an advantage again after a forced sequence of moves.

### Skewer
`theme:skewer` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/skewer)

Skewer: A motif involving a high value piece being attacked, moving out the way, and allowing a lower value piece behind it to be captured or attacked, the inverse of a pin.

### Smothered mate
`theme:smotheredMate` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/smotheredMate)

Smothered mate: A checkmate delivered by a knight in which the mated king is unable to move because it is surrounded (or smothered) by its own pieces.

### Trapped piece
`theme:trappedPiece` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/trappedPiece)

Trapped piece: A piece is unable to escape capture as it has limited moves.

### Underpromotion
`theme:underPromotion` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/underPromotion)

Underpromotion: Promotion to a knight, bishop, or rook.

### X-Ray attack
`theme:xRayAttack` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/xRayAttack)

X-Ray attack: A piece attacks or defends a square, through an enemy piece.

### Zugzwang
`theme:zugzwang` · [source](https://lichess.org/training/zugzwang)

Zugzwang: The opponent is limited in the moves they can make, and all moves worsen their position.
