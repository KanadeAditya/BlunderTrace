Three habits that cost you games

I.

You leave pieces undefended

9 of 20 games

Why it happens

You move a piece to a square where nothing defends it, and your opponent takes it for free. In your games it usually happens right after a capture: the piece that just captured is left standing alone.

How to avoid it

Before every move, ask one question: after this, what can my opponent capture — and is it defended?Retrieved passage · used for this advice“hanging: Unprotected and exposed to capture. A hanging piece may also be said to be en prise.”Glossary of chess — Wikipedia If the answer is “something, and no”, choose a different move. Pieces that protect each other are hard to win.Retrieved passage · used for this advice“When one of your pieces is placed so that it attacks a square occupied by another of your pieces, the former is said to be guarding the latter. When your opponent captures the guarded piece, you can recapture with the guarding piece.”Chess/Tactics, “Guarding” — Wikibooks
